# Nexus Dungeon Master Art Direction

## Master Imagen Style Prefix

Original Nexus Dungeon Master retro-fantasy workstation illustration, Adam Douglas homemade world-building software aesthetic, practical terminal-based dungeon tool gradually enhanced with scanned notebook drawings and graphical modules, fine etched technical line art, old scientific field-guide drawing meets fantasy cartography, Mesahara archaeological and elemental mythology, impossible frozen desert ruins, charcoal and black-green base, muted phosphor mint and cyan linework, restrained warm amber and brass highlights, subtle parchment accents, subtle imperfect analog CRT texture, sophisticated atmospheric restraint, no cyberpunk, no Matrix code, no glossy sci-fi, no corporate SaaS, no ornate medieval HUD, no generic D&D fantasy, no childish cartoon style, no excessive neon, no embedded UI text, no fake labels, no numbers, no English words, no watermark.

## Visual North Star

The approved reference is `references/ui/nexus-dm-workbench-reference.png`.

The strongest reusable traits are:

- Near-black charcoal workstation surface.
- Soft phosphor green, muted cyan, and warm amber/brass accents.
- Thin technical borders and terminal-like visual discipline.
- Hand-drawn fantasy line art embedded in software modules.
- Subtle CRT atmosphere without heavy glitch effects.
- Mesahara as a layered frozen desert of ruins, gates, observatories, scarabs, feline guardians, elemental shrines, and dimensional architecture.

## Asset Rules

- UI text remains HTML/CSS. Generated assets should contain no readable text.
- Icons, glyphs, creatures, objects, portraits, overlays, and marks should prefer transparent PNG.
- Adventure and environment thumbnails may use WebP/PNG with full backgrounds.
- Exit is always positive: green/mint doorway, gate, portal, ascending threshold, completion, or safe progression.
- Boss is separate from Exit and may use danger language; Exit must not be a red X.

## Quality Rejection Rules

Reject and regenerate assets that:

- Drift into Matrix hacker UI, cyberpunk, glossy sci-fi, or corporate SaaS.
- Look like generic D&D or polished Blizzard-style concept art where a workstation illustration is needed.
- Become childish, toy-like, or mobile-game cartoon.
- Use excessive neon or saturated colors.
- Include embedded text, fake labels, numbers, UI buttons, or watermarks.
- Use inconsistent line weight across the starter kit.
- Give the Exit red, error, failure, death, or X-shaped meaning.
- Fail the requested background mode, especially transparent-background icons.
